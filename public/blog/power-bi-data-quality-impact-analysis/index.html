Which Power BI reports break when a table is bad?

A Power BI report is rarely wired straight to a table. It sits at the end of a chain, and a problem in the source flows through every step of that chain into the numbers a business user sees.

The chain usually looks like this:

  • Warehouse table (for example BANKING.CUSTOMER_ACCOUNTS on Snowflake or Oracle)
  • Semantic model, also called a dataset, that queries the table
  • One or more reports built on that semantic model
  • Dashboards, apps, and subscriptions that surface the report

Late data, duplicate keys, or missing values at the table level do not stay at the table level. They ripple forward into every report downstream. To answer which reports break, you need that chain stored as data you can query, not as knowledge that lives in one engineer's head.

The reports that hurt the most when they break are the ones with an audience that acts on them: regulatory filings, board packs, and finance reports. Those are the ones to map first.

Impact analysis runs in the opposite direction to debugging

Debugging starts at the wrong number and works back to the table. Impact analysis starts at the failed check and works forward to the reports. Same chain, read the other way, so you warn people instead of chasing them.

What does Power BI's own lineage view give you?

The Power BI service ships a lineage view for each workspace. It draws the connections between data sources, semantic models, reports, and dashboards, and it offers an impact analysis panel on a semantic model that lists the downstream reports.

This is genuinely useful for planned change. Before you edit or retire a dataset, the lineage view answers the question what breaks if I change this.

  • It shows data source to semantic model to report to dashboard connections inside a workspace
  • Its impact analysis panel lists downstream items for a selected semantic model
  • It is a design-time and change-management tool, driven by a person clicking through it
Where the native lineage view stops

The lineage view does not know that a table failed a uniqueness check at 06:12, or which pipeline job caused it. It is a static picture you go and look at, not an alert that comes to you. Nobody is staring at the lineage view at 6am, so a live failure still reaches the report owner first.

So the native view gives you the map. What it cannot give you is the trigger. To warn owners proactively you have to connect that map to the thing that knows a check just failed, and to the thing that knows which job loaded the table.

In practice: The uniqueness failure nobody saw until 9am

At 06:12 the nightly ODI job nightly_load stops on an Oracle error while loading BANKING.CUSTOMER_ACCOUNTS. A uniqueness check on account_id drops to 98.7 percent.

Three Power BI reports read that table: an executive KPI report, a regulatory exposure report, and a branch performance report. All three now show duplicated accounts.

The finance lead opens the KPI report at 08:55, sees a total that cannot be right, and pings the data team. The morning is spent working backwards from the report to the table to the job.

The failure was known at 06:12. The people who needed it found out at 08:55. Impact analysis closes that gap by carrying the report list inside the alert, so owners hear it before they open the report.

How do you join data quality checks to report lineage?

Useful impact analysis stitches three facts together at the moment a check fails. On their own each fact is half a story. Together they turn a raw failure into a message a report owner can act on.

  1. Anchor on the table
    Everything joins on the fully qualified table name, for example BANKING.CUSTOMER_ACCOUNTS. The check result, the pipeline run, and the report lineage all reference the same table.
  2. Attach the cause
    Link the failed check to the Airflow or Oracle ODI run that loaded the table, with its error message. Now the alert can say what broke, not just that something broke.
  3. Attach the impact
    Attach the list of Power BI reports that read the table, and their owners, from lineage. Now the alert can name the reports that are wrong right now.
  4. Route to both audiences
    Send one alert to the data engineers who fix the source and to the report owners who hold or annotate the report. Each side gets the part they need.

How do you warn report owners before they open a bad report?

The whole point of impact analysis is timing. If the report owner hears about the problem after they have opened the report and acted on it, you have documentation, not prevention. The goal is to reach them in the window between the check failing and the report being read.

  1. Tag your critical reports
    Mark regulatory, board, and finance reports as critical. These get the loudest routing and the tightest expectations.
  2. Monitor their source tables
    Put freshness, volume, completeness, and uniqueness checks on the tables those reports depend on, so a problem is caught at the source.
  3. Alert owners, not just engineers
    When an upstream check fails, route the alert to the report owner in Slack or Microsoft Teams alongside the data team, with the report names spelled out.
  4. Record the incident
    Capture what failed, what it affected, and when it was fixed. That record helps the next audit and the next post-mortem.
What a good alert reads like

Uniqueness check failed on BANKING.CUSTOMER_ACCOUNTS at 98.7 percent. Cause: ODI job nightly_load failed with ORA-01400. Affected: 3 Power BI reports, owners tagged. Policy: BCBS 239, Principle 3. The owner reads that at 06:13 and holds the report, instead of finding it at 08:55.
