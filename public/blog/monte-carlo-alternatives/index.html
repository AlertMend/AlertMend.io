How does Monte Carlo price data observability?

Monte Carlo is one of the best-known data observability platforms, and it is usually the incumbent buyers compare everything else against. It lists tiers such as Start, Scale, Enterprise and Business Critical, and meters usage with credits charged per monitor. List prices are not published, so you request a quote.

Higher tiers add the controls larger teams need: single sign-on and SCIM, more API calls, faster support response, and, at the top, a dedicated instance. That is a normal enterprise ladder. The friction buyers report is not the ladder, it is the meter.

  • Billing unit: the monitor, on a consumption model, quoted privately (Monte Carlo pricing, as of September 2026).
  • Common complaint across the category: per-monitor or usage-based bills are hard to predict as coverage grows.
  • Security and scale features sit on the upper tiers, which is worth mapping to your must-haves before comparing sticker numbers.
Why teams look for an alternative

Three reasons come up again and again: price predictability as coverage grows, a need to self-host or keep data inside the network, and wanting monitoring that starts from a written data quality policy rather than automatic anomaly detection alone.

In practice: The same failure, priced two ways

A retail bank adds uniqueness, completeness and freshness checks to BANKING.CUSTOMER_ACCOUNTS on Snowflake, then rolls the same pattern out to 40 more tables.

On a per-monitor plan, each new check is another billable monitor, so the forecast for full policy coverage keeps moving.

On a per-dataset plan with unlimited checks, the 40 tables set the price and the checks per table do not change the bill.

The billing unit, monitor versus dataset, decides whether covering your whole policy is a budget line or a surprise. Read the pricing page for the unit before the number.

How does each tool approach data quality?

Price is only half the decision. The bigger difference is where monitoring starts and who owns the runtime. Three broad approaches show up.

  1. Anomaly-first SaaS
    Monte Carlo and Metaplane lean on automatic monitors for freshness, volume, nulls and distribution, so you get coverage fast with little setup. Metaplane charges per monitored table and adds column-level lineage and dbt job monitoring on paid plans (Metaplane pricing, as of September 2026).
  2. Platform-plus-catalog SaaS
    Sifflet bundles monitoring with a data catalog and lineage and sells by monitored asset, with self-hosted deployment on its top tier (Sifflet pricing, as of September 2026). It suits teams that want discovery and monitoring in one place.
  3. Checks-as-code and open source
    Soda centres on checks and data contracts that engineers version in code, with a Team plan listed at $750 per month (Soda pricing, as of September 2026). Great Expectations and Elementary give open-source cores with no license fee, moving the cost into engineering time to write, run and maintain the tests.
  4. Policy-driven
    AlertMend starts from your written data quality policy. You upload it as a PDF, AlertMend proposes checks that each cite the clause they enforce, and a person approves every one before it goes live. This suits regulated teams that have to show auditors how each clause is enforced.

How do you choose the right one for your team?

Work through four questions in order. The answers usually point at one or two tools without a scoring spreadsheet.

  1. Do you need predictable spend?
    If full coverage is the goal, prefer a per-table or per-dataset unit with unlimited checks over per-monitor credits, so adding checks does not add cost.
  2. Are you regulated or on-prem?
    Check where the runtime lives, whether credentials leave your network, and whether every check change is audited and reversible. Open-source cores and in-network agents keep more inside your walls.
  3. Are you engineering-led?
    If you have the people to write and run tests, Great Expectations, Elementary or Soda give control at the cost of maintenance time. If you do not, a SaaS with automatic monitors gets you further faster.
  4. Is monitoring policy-driven?
    If auditors ask how each clause is enforced, choose a tool that links checks to clauses. That is the gap AlertMend was built to fill on Snowflake and Oracle.
